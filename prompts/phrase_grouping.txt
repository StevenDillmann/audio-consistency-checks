
INTRODUCTION

You are contributing to a benchmark designed to evaluate audio-language models on their ability to understand how something is spoken, not just what is said.
The benchmark consists of ambiguity-resolution tasks that require models to use prosodic cues in a target sentence to choose the correct follow-up sentence among three plausible options.
A text-only model should find all follow-ups equally likely, while an audio-aware model should be able to disambiguate based on prosody.

REQUIREMENTS FOR THIS TEST CASE

1. Input Sentence

Length: 6–14 words

Must contain at least two possible prosodic groupings that yield different meanings

Must sound plausible and natural in the specified setting

Grouping ambiguity must affect scope or attachment of a modifier, referent, or action

The third reading can be more vague or flattened to simulate models that miss chunking

2. Follow-up Sentences (3 total)

Length: 5–12 words each

Must all be grammatically and contextually plausible in the setting

Only one should follow logically from each prosodic grouping

At least one should interpret a different scope or referent

Follow-ups must not all work if the prosody is removed

3. Prosody Instructions

For each follow-up:

Provide input prosody instruction: how to speak the ambiguous input to yield the correct grouping

Provide follow-up prosody instruction: how to speak the follow-up to reinforce the intended meaning

Use imperative tone, capitalizing pause locations (e.g., “Insert a pause after 'MEN'.”)

Mention pitch shape, pacing, boundary cues (e.g., reset, final lengthening)

4. Explanations

For each follow-up, explain why that specific phrasing makes it correct

Focus on semantic interpretation, not just timing (e.g., attachment scope or subject reference)

5. Voice Assignment

Set "different_voices": true if this works better as dialogue

Set "different_voices": false if a single narrator is most natural

VALIDATION CHECKLIST
- Arrays (options_text, input_instructions, options_instructions, explanations) each length 3
- input_stress_targets = exactly three words actually present in the input sentence
- Index alignment holds for all arrays (i = 0,1,2)
- Each option is only valid under its associated phrasing; it becomes misleading, incoherent, or less likely with other phrasings
- Interpretive differences are structural, not just affective or stylistic (e.g., attachment, scope, or referent)
- Setting is natural, and all options are contextually appropriate
